    What "growth" is here

    Earnings growth year on year, from yfinance (earningsGrowth) — and when the vendor does not report that, revenue growth (revenueGrowth) instead. One column, two possible sources, and the row does not say which one it used.

    That fallback is why the numbers can look extreme. Earnings are a difference, so growth measured off a small or depressed base year runs to hundreds or thousands of percent without the business having changed by anything like that much: a company earning 0.05 a share last year and 0.70 this year is up 1,300%, and that is arithmetic about a base, not a description of a business. Revenue growth almost never does this, so two rows in this column can be quantities of different scales.

    Rows where this is likely carry a base-effect flag next to the number. The axis on the scatter is clipped to the 5th–95th percentile for the same reason — otherwise three base-effect names set the scale and the other hundred sit in a stripe.

    An empty cell is absent, not zero. The vendor reported neither figure, so this name was never judged by any lens that tests growth — it is listed in the unscreenable count under the results, not silently dropped and not scored 0%.

    What “AI shadow debt” means on this screen

    The on-BS debt/equity % that vendors report is the on-balance-sheet leg only. It is a real number and it is incomplete.

    AI data-centre buildouts are increasingly financed through SPVs, joint ventures and project finance — structures that keep large project liabilities off the sponsor's primary consolidating statements. The public archetype is Meta's ~$27B Hyperion campus in Richland Parish, Louisiana, funded through a JV with Blue Owl in which Meta retains roughly a 20% stake and leases the campus back: Meta effectively drives the project, while the project debt sits on the SPV rather than on Meta's balance sheet. So a low reported D/E sitting next to an SPV-sponsor tag is the point of this frame, not a contradiction in it.

    Four editorial buckets mark where to look, one per leg of the financing bet:

    • spv_sponsor — hyperscaler / platform that can park project debt in an SPV or JV below the consolidation threshold.
    • capex_burn — AI-native spender whose cash is being drained by infrastructure; leverage can rise while reported D/E still looks tame.
    • supply_chain — chips, servers, equipment selling into the buildout; less sponsor risk, high cyclical risk if the financing stalls.
    • grid_power — power, cooling and electrical capacity. Watts bound the whole stack.

    These tags are editorial study objects. They are not measured off-balance-sheet notionals, not scraped from 10-K footnotes, not a claim that every tagged name runs a Hyperion-class vehicle today, and not a trading signal. Nothing here is investment advice.

    What an arrangement does

    Arrangements rearrange what is already on the board. One pane keeps the first placed chart and parks the rest; stack gives every chart the full width, one above another. No pane is squeezed below its readable height — when a column asks for more height than the board has, the board grows and the page scrolls. Width is whatever the board itself has, so a row split past what fits shares the shortfall across every pane in it rather than starving the last one.
