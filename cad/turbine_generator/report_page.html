Recommendation. Build configuration A. It is the only layout that (a) takes the only generator with published data that reaches ~70 % water-to-DC efficiency at 1 m/s, (b) keeps the reviewed rotor and its 0.25 hub ratio, (c) puts the generator losses straight into a water-cooled aluminium cap, and (d) uses one small, standard, serviceable rotary seal with a hardened track. It misses the 76 mm depth target by 2.5x; configuration B (139.5 mm, 1.8x) is the smallest layout that keeps the same sealing quality, and it is fully modelled here as the fallback if portability outranks the ~10 % energy penalty and the thermal margin it gives up. Neither layout can reach 76 mm with any 22-30 mm long generator, dry bearings, a rotary seal and a nose cone in series (section 5.5). The GB5015 is not recommended as the generator: no KV, resistance, current rating or thermal data is published, its "220 mN m @ 14.8 V" figure (if it is the usual stall-torque meaning) implies a motor constant too low to absorb even the 1 m/s torque, and no IP65 variant could be found. If one is already on hand, measure it (section 2.4) and keep it only if Kt/sqrt(R_LL) >= 0.11 N m/sqrt(W).

2. Generator selection

2.1 What the GB5015 listing gives and what it does not

Published (Copterlab / SMC Powers pages, via search snippets - re-check on the live page): body 49 x 29 mm, stator 43 x 15 mm, 24N28P, "220 mN m @ 14.8 V", 12-24 V input, hollow shaft "supports slip ring" (bore ~12.5 mm inferred, not stated), M4 mounting, mass quoted as 120-165 g on different pages, $38-55. Not published: KV / back-EMF constant, phase or line resistance, inductance, continuous and peak current, bearing type and load rating, wire gauge, lead time. No IP65 page exists; the nearest sealed gimbal motors (CubeMars GL) are IP45. An IP rating is irrelevant inside a sealed housing anyway: the housing and shaft seal carry the sealing duty.

2.2 Generator model and convention

Hobby convention: open-circuit line-to-line peak voltage E = rpm / KV; torque constant Kt = 60 / (2 pi KV) N m per ampere of rectifier DC current; copper loss = I_dc^2 x R_LL where R_LL is the resistance measured between two motor leads; V_dc = E - I_dc R_LL - 0.8 V (two Schottky drops). Iron, bearing and commutation-inductance losses are folded into a 0.5 W allowance; gimbal motors have 3-5 mH of inductance, so at 100-150 Hz electrical the reactance is comparable to R and the real DC voltage will be a little lower than the table - measure under load. The decisive figure of merit is the motor constant Km = Kt / sqrt(R_LL): copper loss = (torque / Km)^2. To lose less than 20 % of the 1 m/s power (0.18 N m) needs Km >= 0.17 N m/sqrt(W); to absorb 0.72 N m at 2 m/s with less than 15 W loss needs Km >= 0.19.

Cogging: GL60-class peak cogging is ~15-25 mN m (CubeMars quotes 2.4 -> 1.5 cN m for the GL60 II; units are ambiguous on reseller pages), GB5015-class ~2-10 mN m. Thermal: the GL60 is rated 0.6 N m continuous at 1.35 A in free air; at 2 m/s the turbine wants 0.66 N m and 1.45 A (11.6 W copper). With the stator bolted to the aluminium rear cap in flowing water the winding rise is ~10-15 K (section 4.6), so the 2 m/s point is thermally acceptable; the charger must still current-limit at 1.5 A so that a 3 m/s flood cannot push 3 A (46 W) into the winding - the rotor then simply runs at a higher tip-speed ratio.

2.4 Measurements required on the physical generator (before the caps are machined)

  1. KV: spin with a drill at a known rpm (optical tachometer, or rpm = 60 x f / pole-pairs from the AC frequency) and read the line-to-line voltage on all three pairs; KV = rpm / (1.414 x V_LL,rms).
  2. R_LL between each pair of leads (4-wire, or 0.5-1 A DC injection); expect 4.6-5.5 ohm (GL60).
  3. Inductance (LCR meter) - sets the voltage droop at speed.
  4. Cogging torque (thread on a drum + spring scale over a full turn) and, later, total breakaway with the seal fitted.
  5. Bell-face bolt pattern (PCD, thread, depth) and stator-base pattern - these drive a_bell_pcd, a_motor_pcd, a_motor_hole in make_assembly.py.
  6. Overall diameter and length, bore, axial play of the bell, mass.
  7. 30-60 min loaded run at 0.4 N m in air logging voltage, current and winding resistance rise.

3. Generator architectures

3.1 The three (plus one) candidates

  • A. Stationary generator nacelle behind the rotor (recommended, fully modelled): rotor on a 12 mm 316 shaft; shaft crosses the front cap through one lip seal; two sealed stainless bearings on the dry side; jaw coupling to the GL60 bell; stator on the rear cap; WetLink cable exit; three NACA struts on a printed clamp ring carry the nacelle to the duct.
  • B. Hub-integrated outrunner (fully modelled as the compact fallback): the hub is a rotating anodised aluminium tube with two machined O-ring caps; the generator stator sits on a stationary hollow 316 spindle that enters through the rear cap via one lip seal on a Speedi-Sleeve; two bearings on the spindle inside the dry drum carry the rotor; the bell drives the drum front cap through a compliant TPU disc; cable runs inside the spindle and is potted at the strut boss.
  • C. Magnetic coupling with a fully static-sealed cartridge (upgrade path, not modelled): A's cartridge with the front cap replaced by a non-conductive cup and a coaxial magnetic coupling; wet ceramic bearings carry the rotor.
  • D. Flooded, potted generator (Blue Robotics T200 practice): no enclosure at all; stator epoxy-potted, coated magnets, water-lubricated bearings. Rejected for a generator in silty water: the T200's plastic bearings deform when run dry and had a continuous-duty wear recall; potting a 69 mm stator reliably is a process the team would have to qualify; magnet corrosion is unforgiving.

3.3 Why A

B's single real advantage is 50 mm of length. For that it gives up 10 % of rotor power (hub ratio), excludes the only generator with adequate published data (the GL60 does not fit a hub), halves the thermal margin, and adds an over-constrained bell mount (the motor's own bearings and the drum bearings both locate the bell; the TPU disc absorbs the mismatch but has to be tuned). Its sealing is as good as A's, not better - which is the point of using the same machined-cap/Speedi-Sleeve solution in both. C is the right second iteration once A has run: the same tube, rear cap and generator carry over, only the front cap becomes a non-conductive cup with a coupling, so the 13-26 % starting-torque tax and the only wearing seal disappear. The magnetic-coupling research (research/research_magcoupling.md) found no COTS coupling with published data in exactly this class except laboratory stirrer heads (0.2-0.9 N m); a DIY coaxial coupling of 8-12 N42 magnets at a 3-4 mm gap is estimated at 0.8-1.5 N m pull-out, must be bench-tested, and a face (disc) coupling is not acceptable because it pulls the halves together with ~80-120 N, which would load the bearings and bow a plastic wall.

4. Waterproof enclosure engineering (configuration A)

4.1 The boundary

Anodised 6061-T6 tube (80 x 2.5 mm, 95 mm long) closed by two machined caps. Each cap is a flange outside the tube and an 18 mm plug inside it carrying two radial O-rings 7 mm apart (the second is redundancy and a wiper for silt). The only rotating crossing is the 12 mm shaft through the front cap: one spring-loaded lip seal running on an SKF Speedi-Sleeve. The cable leaves through a Blue Robotics WetLink M10 penetrator in the rear cap (static, FKM-sealed, rated 95 m for the HC version). An M6 vent port in the rear cap is used for the vacuum leak test and closed with an O-ring plug. The caps are retained by three M3 radial 316 screws each, placed on the dry side of the inner O-ring so the screw holes never see water. The section view cad_out/A_section_view.png draws the boundary in red.

4.2 Static O-ring glands (Parker O-Ring Handbook values, verified in the research notes)

Cross-section 2.62 mm (AS568 -1xx): radial gland depth 2.06-2.11 mm (17-24 % squeeze), groove width 3.56-3.61 mm, groove corner radii 0.2-0.4 mm, groove surface Ra <= 1.6 um, bore Ra <= 0.8 um with a 15 deg x 1.5 mm lead-in chamfer on the tube. Plug diameter 74.9 mm in a 75.0 (+0.2/-0) bore gives 0.05-0.15 mm diametral clearance (extrusion is not a concern at 0.3 bar). Groove OD 70.7 mm takes an AS568-148 (69.5 mm ID) EPDM 70 ring at 1.8 % stretch. EPDM is the right material for continuous fresh water (NBR is acceptable at 0-25 C but ages faster in water; FKM is fine cold); lubricate EPDM only with silicone or PFPE grease. Measure the real tube bore and cut the groove depth to it - stock tube ID is +-0.2 mm. The caps may be Delrin (acetal) instead of aluminium for the front cap; the rear cap must be aluminium because it is the generator's heat sink.

4.3 Rotary seal

Lip seal 12 x 22 x 7 mm, single lip with garter spring, lip facing the water (the pressure side), bore 22 H8 in the cap, 7 mm deep from the wet face. 316 is too soft for a lip-seal track (HRB 83; SKF asks for >= 45 HRC for long life in contaminated media), so the shaft wears an SKF Speedi-Sleeve 99047 (hardened stainless, 0.28 mm wall) at the seal position - Speedi-Sleeves exist from 11.99 mm up, which is why the shaft is 12 mm and not 10 mm. Pressure: a standard oil seal holds 0.3-0.5 bar; our maximum is 0.2 bar at 2 m depth. Friction: no published figure exists for this size; SKF's model (T = (F_lub + F_material) x d/2) with a 3-8 N lip force gives 4-24 mN m running and about twice that at breakaway. A PTFE lip (Dichta C-O or Trelleborg Varilip PDR, $40-120) halves it; a V-ring is not a pressure seal; ferrofluid seals fail in water. The seal is the one wearing part: plan to replace it at 6-12 months, inspect at 2 weeks.

4.4 Bearings and alignment

Two 6001-2RS 440C stainless bearings (12 x 28 x 8) back-to-back in a 28 H7 bore, dry side, grease-sealed. Thrust (46 N at 2 m/s, 110 N in a 3 m/s flood) passes shaft -> DIN 471 circlip -> rear bearing inner race -> balls -> outer race -> DIN 472-28 internal circlip -> cap -> tube -> struts. ISO 281 L10 at 46 N axial + 22 N radial is > 10^6 h: fatigue is irrelevant, contamination and corrosion decide bearing life, which is why they sit behind the seal. The jaw coupling (D20 x L25, 98A spider) absorbs the mismatch between the shaft axis (defined by the front cap) and the GL60 bell axis (defined by its own bearings in the stator on the rear cap): 0.2 mm radial, 1 deg angular. Rotor overhang from the front bearing is 35 mm; bending stress in the 12 mm shaft is < 5 MPa, torsion < 8 MPa at the 1.5 N m jam case; the 3 mm 316 cross pin sees 22 MPa in double shear (316 shear strength ~300 MPa).

4.5 Materials, corrosion, fasteners

Tube and caps 6061-T6 hard-anodised (type III) and sealed; 316 shaft, collar, pin, screws; 440C bearings (dry side); EPDM O-rings; FKM penetrator seal. Galvanic pairs that matter in fresh water: 316 screws in aluminium (~670 mV apart) - coat the threads with Tef-Gel and use nylon washers under the heads; keep brass heat-set inserts out of wetted aluminium (use them only in the printed clamp ring and struts, or use 316 through-bolts with nyloc nuts, which the CAD does). The Speedi-Sleeve is 302 stainless on a 316 shaft: acceptable. No dissimilar-metal contact inside the dry volume matters.

4.6 Thermal management

GL60 copper loss is 0.8 / 4.5 / 11.6 W at 1 / 1.5 / 2 m/s (current-limited to 1.5 A, 12 W, in a flood). Heat path: winding -> stator core -> aluminium rear cap (bolted, thermal paste) -> water. The cap's wet face (0.005 m2) with h ~ 800 W/m2K in moving water rises < 4 K at 12 W; the stator-to-cap joint adds ~1 K/W -> winding ~15-20 K above the water. The air volume inside stays cool; the front cap and the seal see no generator heat. If a GM5208 (13.7 ohm) were used instead, copper loss at 2 m/s is 19 W and the same path still holds the winding below 50 K rise.

4.7 Pressure, breathing and leak paths

External pressure (0.03-0.2 bar) pushes both caps into the tube and the lip onto the shaft: the geometry is self-energising. Breathing: a 20 K temperature swing changes the internal air pressure by 7 %; the lip seal will relieve an internal over-pressure (it lifts) and resists under-pressure (it tightens), so a long cold soak after a warm assembly pulls the lip harder onto the sleeve - assemble at or below water temperature, or fit the vent plug after a few minutes in the water. Leak paths in order of likelihood: (1) lip seal (wear, scored sleeve, backwards installation), (2) penetrator (wrong cable size, under-torqued), (3) O-rings (nicked at assembly, groove burr, wrong size for the measured bore), (4) anodising pores on a cap face (negligible with two rings). A desiccant bag and a moisture-indicator card inside make the first three detectable at the 2-week inspection.

4.8 Assembly / disassembly

Generator cartridge = tube + rear cap + GL60 + adapter: assemble on the bench, vacuum-test, then slide the front cap (with shaft, bearings, seal pre-assembled) in, fit the three M3 screws, vacuum-test again. To swap the generator: unclamp the strut ring (4 x M4), pull the whole cartridge out of the duct, remove the three rear-cap screws and withdraw the rear cap with the motor on it. To swap the seal: pull the rotor (cross pin + grub screws), remove the collar and Speedi-Sleeve, press out the seal from the dry side after removing the front cap. Nothing requires breaking a bonded joint.

Radial loads: unbalance 2 N at runaway with 0.5 mm eccentricity, shear-flow asymmetry ~5 N and 0.23 N m yaw moment (10 % thrust asymmetry assumed). Front bearing ~22 N radial, rear ~15 N.

5.2 Rotor

Unchanged from the reviewed revision 2 except the shaft bore (10 -> 12 mm to allow the Speedi-Sleeve). The hub's blind bore, 3.2 mm cross pin and two M4 grub screws remain the torque path; the nose cone is integral. Printed PETG/ASA, 4+ perimeters, balanced to 0.5 g.

5.3 Nacelle, struts, duct interface

Nacelle 80 mm OD x 95 mm behind a 3 mm gap from the hub. Three NACA 0015 struts (chord 18 mm, 2.7 mm thick) on a printed split clamp ring at 45 % of the tube length, i.e. ~55 mm behind the rotor plane, 120 deg apart and clocked 60 deg from the blades' rest position. Outer pads 30 x 22 mm with two M4 holes on a 206 mm duct bore (2 mm tip clearance); a 40 mm long reference duct ring is included in the CAD only to show the interface - replace it with your duct. The cable runs in a groove along one strut.

5.4 Effect of the nacelle and struts on the blades

The nacelle (80 mm) is larger than the hub (50.8 mm): its frontal area is 15 % of the rotor disk, all of it in the hub wake. Actuator-disk reasoning puts the induced velocity deficit at the blade root at a few percent and at mid-span at < 1 %; the three struts (18 x 2.7 mm) are 55 mm downstream of the rotor and block < 2 % of the annulus, producing a weak 3-per-revolution load ripple on the blades. Expected effect on Cp: -2 to -4 %, within the BEM's own uncertainty (reviewed as +-0.02). **No new BEM or CFD is required before the prototype**; measure rpm and DC power against water speed at the first deployment and compare with section 7. If a later iteration shrinks the nacelle (e.g. a 60 mm generator), the penalty halves.

7.2 Starting

Static rotor torque at 1 m/s is 0.091 N m. Breakaway budget: seal 2 x 12 = 24 mN m + bearings 6 + GL60 cogging ~24 = 54 mN m, i.e. 60 % of what the water offers - it should start, but not with a stiff seal, a scored sleeve or an un-greased bearing. Measure breakaway on the bench (VALIDATION.md 2.1) and keep it under 45 mN m; a PTFE seal or a GB5015-class low-cogging motor each buy ~15 mN m. Below ~0.9 m/s expect no self-start.

7.4 Load matching

A PM generator into a rectifier and a resistive or battery load has a torque that rises with speed, which keeps the rotor within 3 % of peak Cp from 1 to 2 m/s (verified in the blade review). An MPPT boost charger does the same actively. A constant-torque or constant-current load sized at 1.5 m/s stalls the rotor at 1 m/s. Set the charger's current limit at 1.5 A (GL60) so that it can never stall the rotor and the winding cannot exceed 12 W; above 2 m/s the rotor then drifts up its Cp curve harmlessly.

8. Validation

VALIDATION.md gives the full procedure: bench measurements of the motor, shaft and caps; static vacuum (-70 kPa, 15 min, < 1 kPa drift) and 24 h submersion tests of the sealed cartridge with a moisture card; thermal cycling; rotating tests at 650 and 1300 rpm submerged with breakaway-torque measurement; electrical verification of KV, resistance and the loss model; insulation resistance; in-water commissioning with rpm and DC power logged against water speed; and the service intervals.
