### 1.2 Torque-capacity estimate method (DIY face and coaxial coupling)

**Sources for the method.** Closed-form axial-coupling torque/force expressions: Lubin, Mezani, Rezzoug, "Simple analytical expressions for the force and torque of axial magnetic couplings", IEEE TEC 2012 - open PDF (https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00673920/file/final_axialcoupling.pdf); variables R1, R2 (magnet inner/outer radii), e (air gap), h (magnet thickness), alpha (pole-arc ratio), p (pole pairs), Br - verified. Their 3 mm-gap, 6-pole-pair example gives ~90 N m (model), and a related prototype 60 N m at 4 mm gap - verified (https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01444195/file/ICEM2016-final.pdf); "maximum torque almost halves when the air gap goes from 2 mm to 7 mm" - verified (https://www.emworks.com/application/axial-flux-permanent-magnet-couplings-transforming-contactless-power-transmission). Dolisy et al. 3-D formula (https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01164388). Radial (coaxial) couplings: Furlani, Wang, Kusnadi, IEEE Trans. Magn. 1995, DOI 10.1109/20.406554 (integral form) - verified (https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10032242). Vendor calculator (disc + coaxial, includes eddy loss): https://radialmagnet.com/magnetic-coupling-torque-calculator/ - states torque scales with B^2 ("5 % loss of Br costs ~10 % of pull-out torque"), recommends **pull-out = 1.5-2x worst-case load at max magnet temperature and max gap** - verified. Supermagnete pull-force data for 10x5 N42: **approx. 2.6 kg (25.5 N) to steel, 0.52 kg displacement force; epoxy-coated version 2 kg (19.6 N)** - verified (https://www.supermagnete.fi/eng/disc-magnets-neodymium/disc-magnet-10mm-5mm_S-10-05-N, https://www.supermagnete.se/eng/disc-magnets-neodymium/disc-magnet-10mm-5mm_S-10-05-E). K&J publishes "Case 3 magnet-to-magnet force vs distance" charts per magnet (e.g. D82 = 1/2 x 1/8 in, 6.44 lb case-1 pull) - verified (https://www.kjmagnetics.com/proddetail.asp?prod=D82); the chart values were not readable here.

**Method A - per-pair force (simplest, DIY).** *estimate*
1. Get F_pair(g) = attraction between one magnet pair across the total gap g (K&J "case 3" chart, Supermagnete calculator, or measure with a scale).
   Dipole check for a 10x5 mm N42 disc (Br ~ 1.3 T, m = Br*V/mu0 = 0.41 A m^2): F = 3*mu0*m^2/(2*pi*r^4) with r = g + 5 mm -> ~15 N at g = 4 mm; the dipole model over-predicts at this spacing, so take **F_pair(4 mm) ~ 8-12 N, F_pair(3 mm) ~ 11-15 N, F_pair(5 mm) ~ 6-9 N** (estimate; contact value ~25 N from the Supermagnete figure above).
2. Maximum tangential restoring force per pair when the discs are displaced by half a pole pitch: F_t,max ~ 0.3-0.5 * F_pair(g) (estimate from dipole geometry; the neighbouring opposite-pole magnet adds a repulsive push of the same sign).
3. Pull-out torque T_po ~ N * F_t,max * R_pcd, N = number of magnets per disc (8-12), R_pcd = pitch-circle radius.
   Worked example: 8 x 10x5 N42, PCD 45 mm, g = 4 mm: T_po ~ 8 * 4 N * 0.0225 m ~ **0.7 N m (range 0.5-1.0 N m)**. 12 x 10x10x5 blocks on PCD 50 mm, g = 3 mm: ~ **1.5-2.5 N m**. These bracket the 0.72 N m running / 1.5 N m jam requirement; use the 1.5-2x margin from radialmagnet.com, i.e. aim for >= 2.2-3 N m pull-out if the coupling must *not* slip in a jam, or ~1.0-1.2 N m if you *want* it to act as the torque fuse at ~1.5 N m.

**Method B - air-gap shear-stress cross-check** (the form used by the Lubin paper and the radialmagnet calculator). *estimate*
T_po ~ k * (B_g^2 / 2 mu0) * A_active * R_mean, k ~ 0.4-0.6 for disc magnets with ~50-60 % pole-arc fill.
Mid-gap flux density of two facing 10x5 N42 discs from the axial-field formula B(z) = (Br/2)[(L+z)/sqrt(R^2+(L+z)^2) - z/sqrt(R^2+z^2)], doubled for two magnets: **~0.66 T at 3 mm gap, ~0.58 T at 4 mm, ~0.50 T at 5 mm** (estimate). With A_active = 8 * 78.5 mm^2 and R_mean = 22.5 mm, the 4 mm case gives ~50 kPa effective shear and T ~ 0.7 N m - consistent with Method A. Typical NdFeB couplings run 30-80 kPa (estimate).

**Coaxial version with the same magnets.** *estimate* Put the 8-12 discs (poles alternating) around a 40-50 mm inner rotor and the mating set inside a cup; T_po ~ k * (B_g^2/2 mu0) * (2 pi R_g L_mag * fill) * R_g. For R_g = 25 mm, L = 10 mm, fill ~0.5, B_g ~0.5 T at 4 mm radial gap: ~0.8-1.2 N m. Radial gap must include wall + two clearances; a cylindrical can is stiffer than a flat disc for the same thickness, which is why industrial sealed couplings are coaxial.

**Axial attraction force (face coupling).** *estimate* At the aligned (zero-load) position every pair attracts: F_axial ~ N * F_pair(g) ~ 8 x 10 N = **~80 N at 4 mm gap, ~100-120 N at 3 mm**, falling as the load angle increases. This force (i) loads a thrust bearing on *both* shafts continuously, (ii) bows a flat non-metallic wall toward the rotor (check deflection: a 50 mm dia, 3 mm acetal disc under ~100 N distributed load deflects of order 0.1-0.3 mm - estimate; support it or use a slightly domed wall), (iii) adds friction torque mu*F*r_bearing on any plain thrust face. A coaxial (radial) coupling has **no net axial force when axially aligned** and **zero net radial force when concentric** (forces cancel around the circumference; only a small axial restoring force appears if the rotors are offset). Patent US 12107474 notes disc couplings are "usually used only for drive ratings up to a maximum of 5 kW" because of the axial magnetic forces - verified (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12107474). The Lubin 2012 paper gives the closed-form axial-force expression if you want the exact number.

### 1.3 Eddy-current losses vs wall material (8-pole coupling, 300-650 rpm)

Published figures: losses "increase with shell diameter, rotational speed and wall thickness" - verified (https://www.worldpumps.com/content/features/energy-efficientcontainment-shells); "316 stainless produces at least twice the eddy losses of Hastelloy C" - verified (https://www.ipesearch.co.uk/page_835119.asp); non-conductive shells (plastic, ceramic): "eddy current losses are totally eliminated", limited by strength/pressure - verified (https://b-k.com/index.php/pump-application-guide/magnetic-drive-pumps/magnetic-drive-pumps-section-2); stainless shell "can cost several percent of transmitted power" vs non-conductive - verified (https://radialmagnet.com/magnetic-couplings-pumps-hvac/); measured: 304 SS can loss at 3000 rpm = **23x** that at 500 rpm (i.e. ~n^1.75-n^2) - verified (https://zzs.ujs.edu.cn/pgjx/CN/abstract/abstract2864.shtml); analytical treatments: Matsumoto & Chabu (ICEM 2014, COMPEL 2017, "major degradation of torque capability" through a conductive wall) - verified (https://www.emeraldinsight.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/COMPEL-12-2016-0573/full/html); Markovic & Perriard, IEEE Trans. Magn. 2007 (rotating magnet in conducting cylinder) - verified (https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/114393). Kyocera ceramic shells: "up to 15 % drive-power reduction" (marketing) - verified (https://www.kyocera-fineceramics.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Download/broschueren/BR_KFEG_Containment_shells_EN.pdf).

**Thin-wall formula** (wall thickness t << skin depth, which holds here: electrical frequency f = p*n/60 = 4 x 650/60 = 43 Hz; skin depth 316 ~ 66 mm, Al ~ 12 mm). *estimate*
P_eddy ~ k_f * sigma * t * A_swept * B_n^2 * v^2, with k_f ~ 0.5 (sinusoidal pole pattern), v = omega*R_mean, A_swept = annulus swept by the magnets, B_n = normal flux density at the wall. Drag torque T_eddy = P/omega (proportional to speed; **zero at stand-still, so it does not affect breakaway, only run-up**).

Worked numbers for the 8 x 10x5 face coupling, R_mean 22.5 mm, A_swept ~1.4e-3 m^2, B_n ~0.45 T at the wall:

| Wall | sigma (S/m) | t | P at 650 rpm (v = 1.53 m/s) | P at 300 rpm | share of 49 W shaft power at 650 rpm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PETG / acetal (POM) / acrylic / PEEK / GFRP / ceramic | ~0 | 1-4 mm | **0 W** | 0 W | 0 % |
| 316 stainless (rho 0.74 uOhm m) | 1.35e6 | 1 mm | ~0.45 W | ~0.1 W | ~1 % |
| 316 stainless | 1.35e6 | 2 mm | ~0.9 W | ~0.2 W | ~2 % |
| 316 stainless | 1.35e6 | 4 mm | ~1.8 W (+ torque loss from larger gap) | ~0.4 W | ~4 % |
| Hastelloy C276 (rho 1.3 uOhm m - verified, https://core.ac.uk/download/147252746.pdf) | 7.7e5 | 2 mm | ~0.5 W | ~0.1 W | ~1 % |
| Aluminium 6061 (rho ~0.04 uOhm m) | 2.5e7 | 1 mm | ~8 W | ~1.8 W | ~16 % |
| Aluminium | 2.5e7 | 2 mm | ~16 W | ~3.5 W | ~33 % |

(All rows estimate; the classical formula is an upper bound that neglects the induced-current reaction field - verified caveat from https://radialmagnet.com/magnetic-coupling-torque-calculator/.)

**Acceptable walls:** any non-conductive wall (acetal/POM, PETG, PEEK, acrylic, GFRP/epoxy) - zero loss, so the wall is sized only for stiffness against the ~100 N axial pull, water absorption and creep (PEEK and POM best; PETG moderate absorption; acrylic crazes). KTR sells PEEK shrouds for exactly this reason. Thin 316 (1-2 mm) is acceptable (~1-2 % loss at 650 rpm) if you need a metal wall; note 1.4462 duplex (KTR standard) and 430 are ferromagnetic and would short part of the flux - prefer austenitic 316/316L (estimate). **Aluminium, copper, brass: unacceptable** (loses a quarter to a third of the shaft power and the drag torque steals run-up margin).

### 1.4 Magnet corrosion protection in freshwater

- Mechanism: in liquid water the Nd-Fe-B matrix phase corrodes (Nd-rich grain-boundary phase stays intact), unlike the selective grain-boundary attack in humid/HAST tests - verified (https://cris.vtt.fi/en/publications/corrosion-mechanisms-of-sintered-nd-fe-b-magnets-in-the-presence-/); crater pitting under water droplets by differential aeration, starting at the Nd-rich phase - verified (https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/environmental-degradation-of-ndfeb-magnets/); hydrogen uptake expands the lattice and cracks grain boundaries - verified (https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/corrrev-2022-0014/html). Plating adhesion failures trace to pre-treatment residues in surface porosity - verified (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5162064).
- Ni-Cu-Ni "is not rust proof", "not waterproof" without a special coating - verified (https://www.kjmagnetics.com/blog.asp?p=rust, https://totalelement.com/blogs/about-neodymium-magnets/are-neodymium-magnets-waterproof).
- K&J salt-water test: in 6-7 weeks of submersion "only one DC2E epoxy coated magnet survived"; a scratched epoxy magnet rusted at an edge away from the scratch; epoxy "not very abrasion resistant" - verified (https://www.kjmagnetics.com/blog.asp?p=rust).
- Options with sources: **epoxy-coated** discs (Supermagnete 10x5 N42 epoxy, 2 kg pull - verified, link above; dip-paint epoxy "stable in moist environments ... saline solutions" - verified https://e-magnetsuk.com/introduction-to-neodymium-magnets/coatings/); **PTFE-coated** N42 discs 10x2, 21x3.5, 28x11.5 mm from first4magnets/magnetexpert ("waterproof PTFE coating") - verified (https://www.first4magnets.com/product/10mm-dia-x-2mm-thick-x-32mm-c-sink-ptfe-coated-n42-neodymium-magnet-11771, https://www.first4magnets.com/product/28mm-dia-x-115mm-thick-ptfe-coated-n42-neodymium-magnet-6kg-pull-19141) - no submersion data found; **plastic-encapsulated** (1 mm ABS or 1/32 in polypropylene shell) - verified (https://totalelement.com/collections/waterproof-plastic-coated-neodymium-magnets); **welded 316/316L stainless housings** (Goudsmit AISI 316L bars, continuous welds, "waterproof", N42SH - verified https://www.goudsmitmagnets.com/solutions/magnetic-filtering-separating/bar-magnets; Blumags 316 or Ti grade-2 subsea housings - verified via search; Ganter GN 52.5 / K&J stainless pot magnets - verified https://ganternorm.com/en/products/quick-finder/GN-52.5-Retaining-Magnets-Neodymium-Iron-Boron-NdFeB-Housing-Stainless-Steel-with-Gummed-Magnetic-Surface, https://kjmagnetics.com/products/stainless-steel-mounting-magnets). Avoid natural-rubber coatings (pinholes) - verified (totalelement link above).
- Practical recommendation (estimate): only the turbine-side half is wet; pot Ni-Cu-Ni + epoxy magnets fully into the printed/machined carrier with a filled epoxy, or close the carrier with a thin welded/glued cover so the magnets sit in their own static-sealed pocket. Keep coating abrasion from silt off the magnets (they never touch anything in a coupling). The generator-side half is inside the dry cartridge and needs only standard plating.

### 1.5 Overload behaviour (slip / torque fuse / resync)

- Confirmed: above the pull-out torque the driven side stops following; "the coupling element connected to the drive shaft continues to rotate, but no longer transmits torque" - verified (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12324513); KTR: "if the maximum coupling torque and the maximum torsion angle are exceeded, the power transmission is interrupted" - verified (globalspec KTR datasheet above). Synchronous couplings have zero slip below pull-out - verified (https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/magnetic-coupling-slip-synchronous-vs-induction-technology).
- Re-synchronisation: "decoupling does not, generally, lead to permanent damage; synchronization is reinitiated at the next magnetic pole coupling point" (Dexter, vendor claim) - verified (https://directindustry.com/prod/dexter-magnetic-technologies/product-23025-356258.html). Lubin, Mezani, Rezzoug 2013 (IEEE TIE) analyse the transient loss of synchronism and pull-in - verified (https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00833962). Pole-slipping produces a pulsating torque at p x (relative speed) - verified (https://avestia.com/CDSR2022_Proceedings/files/paper/CDSR_109.pdf).
- Caution from pump practice: prolonged running decoupled "will permanently demagnetize" the magnets - verified (https://magnatexpumps.com/blog/) - the mechanism is heating (eddy currents in a metallic can, hot process fluid). For this design (non-conductive wall, cold river water, <0.1 T stray field, N42 at <40 C) prolonged slip is thermally harmless (estimate), but the slipping generator half will "ratchet" audibly and the thrust bearing sees the pulsating axial force.
- Pull-in (estimate): re-engagement needs the relative speed to fall low enough that the magnetic restoring torque can accelerate the driven inertia within one pole pitch. With a jammed turbine the generator rotor spins down in well under a second and re-locks as soon as the jam clears; with a seized generator the turbine free-wheels over it and re-locks only when it slows nearly to a stop. Sizing the pull-out to ~1.0-1.2 N m makes the coupling the torque fuse for the 1.5 N m jam case; sizing >= 2.5 N m makes it hold through the jam.

### 2.3 Published experience with sealing small submerged hydro generators

- **WaterLily (Seaformatics):** no teardown or FAQ found. US patents 11788502, 12241447, 12618390 ("Hydroelectric/Fluid turbine system and method of use") describe a disc-stack turbine on a **hollow shaft driving a two-plate magnetic coupler**, with the generator in a sealed shell with O-ring seals, a ball bearing and a thrust bearing, "the use of a magnetic coupler ... provides many benefits, including fluid sealing" - verified (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11788502, https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12241447). **Assignee not confirmed as Seaformatics** (could not open the record); Seaformatics (MUN alumni) said it had "one patent issued and another in progress" - verified (https://mun.ca/physics/shared-news-articles/alumni-turn-heads-with-wind--and-water-powered-electronics-charger.php).
- **Enomad Uno:** marketed IP68, "propeller turns the alternator"; **no teardown or seal information found** (https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20171113-enomad-uno-hydroelectric-power, https://m.reach.dog/shop/mijubuycom/products/enomad-smallest-hydropower-generator).
- **Idenergie (river turbine, 1.5-2 m/s):** "shaftless technology prevents water from leaking into the generator's electric envelope", generator sandwiched between two Darrieus rotors, "designed without penetrating shafts" - verified, vendor claims (https://www.optimistdaily.com/?p=868775, https://www.designboom.com/technology/idenergies-river-sustainable-turbine-river-flow-electricity-04-24-2022/).
- **Waterotor:** only output claims found, no mechanical details. **Turbulent:** nothing relevant returned.
- **PolyU micro cross-flow turbine in water mains (Du et al. 2018; Shen 2023 thesis):** chose a magnetic coupling with containment shroud *because* mechanical seals wear: "the magnetic coupling seals water by a containment shroud and transfers torque ... no friction between the external coupling half and internal coupling half"; ~600 Wh/day at 1.3 m/s - verified (https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/103082/1/Du_Micro_Magnetic_Driven.pdf, https://briefs.techconnect.org/papers/micro-magnetic-driven-bidirectional-turbine-used-in-urban-water-mains-for-hydropower-generation/); later thesis notes the coupling's size/weight penalty - verified (https://theses.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/200/12777).
- **Patent literature on miniature hydro generators:** shaft "through a watertight seal made of rubber" or, alternatively, "a magnetic coupler ... to eliminate the need for penetration of the housing" - verified (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7233078); "longevity arises in submerged generators when the rotary shaft seal wears out"; "sand filled water may cause rapid wear" - verified (https://patents.google.com/patent/US6727600).
- **Uppsala Soderfors river turbine:** direct-drive PM generator in a water-tight housing that also carries the turbine shaft; no seal/bearing details in the excerpts - verified (https://www.uu.se/en/news/2013/2013-03-08-marine-current-power-being-tested-in-river-dalalven).
- Student/hobby builds: SDSU hydrokinetic turbine sealed the shaft with a "waterproof bearing" plus epoxy/JB-Weld/caulk, no leak data - verified (https://mechanical.sdsu.edu/design-day/_files/2020/niwc-hydrokinetic-turbine-poster.pdf); forum consensus "any dynamic seal will leak a little until it wears, and then it will leak a lot" - verified (https://www.eng-tips.com/threads/submerged-axial-flow-hydroturbine.436378/latest).

**Architectural takeaway (estimate):** two architectures dominate the 1-100 W submerged class: (a) fully flooded, epoxy-potted PM generator with water-lubricated polymer plain bearings (Blue Robotics pattern - lowest part count, but magnets/laminations need potting and silt wears the bearings), and (b) turbine -> magnetic coupling through a non-conductive wall -> generator in a static-O-ring-sealed cartridge (WaterLily-patent, PolyU and lab-stirrer pattern - zero seal friction, generator stays dry, costs some volume and the axial pull of a face coupling). A rotary lip seal is the option every source treats as the wear-out item.
